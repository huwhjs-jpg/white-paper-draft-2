The story in four questions

The governing thought, unchanged: Chinese equipment makers won share mainly by redesigning the commercial system around the machine, not by selling it cheaply, and most of their moves can be copied. Chapter 3 adds one finding: in the next products the same moves arrive in a different order, and who the buyer is decides the order.

Chapter 1What happened?Chinese makers became the main suppliers of heavy equipment across India and Southeast Asia. The shift turned twice, in 2022 and 2025, and it is not a commodity boom.
Chapter 2How did they do it?Six moves, in three pairs: get in, win the sale, stay. Each has an example, a limit and a version others can copy.
Chapter 3Where is it heading next?Into the machines factories buy, then robots. Equipment sold through distributors moves more slowly, on price first. How a product is bought sets the pace.
Chapter 4What should you do about it?Know where the playbook breaks, make your first move, and watch five signs.

The central question, as approved: Which of the moves that won Chinese OEMs share can be copied, by whom and how fast, and which product categories will they reach next? Chapter 2 answers "which moves", Chapter 3 "which categories next", and Chapter 4 "by whom and how fast". Chapter 1 shows why the question matters.

3 · Three kinds of buyer

This replaces the six product groups.

  • Contractors and fleets, through dealers: excavators, cranes, forklifts. The original story.
  • Factories: plastic-moulding machines, machine tools, laser cutters, and robots.
  • Plant engineers, off the shelf through distributors: compressors, pumps, generators.

Chapter 1

What happened?

In under a decade Chinese makers became the main suppliers of heavy equipment in India and Southeast Asia. The shift turned twice, and it is structural, not a commodity boom.

1.1
The size of the shiftThree numbers carry it. China's share of Indonesia's excavator imports went from 33% to 75%, and of India's crane imports from 35% to 93%. In 2025 China overtook Japan as the main supplier of robots in all four markets with 2025 data.
EvidenceUN Comtrade, 2019–2025Exhibit1. Where China gained, by country and product
1.2
When it turnedChina's home market collapsed after 2021, and overseas became the main business in 2022. From 2025 Chinese makers began owning the whole system abroad: plants, finance companies, repair.
EvidenceCCMA sales: exports about 10% of units to 2020, about half from 2023; SANY's filings; dated movesExhibit2. The timeline
1.3
Not just a commodity boomKomatsu lost share in Indonesia while the market grew and coal output hit records. China also gained in products that have nothing to do with mining. A plain verdict follows: structural in India and Indonesia, and in most of Thailand.
EvidenceUnited Tractors and Hexindo disclosures; the five tests, with details moved to the appendix
1.4
The open questionWill share bought partly on easy credit survive the 2026–27 downturn? Chapter 4 says what to watch.
EvidenceExports +33.5% in H1 2026; mining-permit delays in Indonesia; payment delays in Indian road building

Chapter 2

How did they do it?

Not by being cheapest. They redesigned how the machine is sold, financed, serviced and made. Each move can be copied, and most are cheaper for incumbents to copy than they were for the Chinese firms that made them first.

Win the sale2. Treat customer credit as part of the product
3. Price just enough, then invest in the lifecycle
Stay4. Localise plants, then export from the host
5. Turn local-content and investment rules into an advantage

The six labels are as approved; only the grouping is new. Each move gets four short beats: what they did, one example, where it stops working, and what others can copy.

2.1
Get inSANY won half of India's long-reach excavator niche, then used the dealers it built to sell everything else. Chinese forklift makers arrived just as buyers switched to electric. Limit: where machines work hardest, buyers go back to brands they trust.
EvidenceSANY India; electric-forklift import shares (60–69%); Tata Hitachi's recovery
2.2
Win the saleSmall contractors cannot borrow from banks, so Chinese makers financed them. SANY went from a 2017 lender agreement to its own licensed finance company in Indonesia in 2026. Prices sat only 0–10% below the competition, and the investment went into parts and repair. Limit: credit-led sales are exposed in a downturn.
EvidenceIndia's crane case; SANY Indonesia; United Tractors' parts and service incomeExhibit3. SANY's finance story
2.3
StayFour Chinese makers build in India, and new plants opened in Indonesia and Thailand in 2025–26. Local plants also earn access to government buying under local-content rules. Limit: plants depend on trade policy, and announced plants are not always real production.
EvidencePlant list; Indonesia's local-content credit; India's investment rulesExhibit4. The moves over time

Chapter 3

Where is it heading next?

Into the machines factories buy, because Chinese factories abroad bring Chinese machines with them, and then into robots. Equipment bought off the shelf through distributors moves more slowly, on price first. How a product is bought decides how long incumbents have before the full Chinese system arrives.

3.1
How a product is bought sets the paceThe three kinds of buyer, and where each product stands: the year China overtook Japan, and which of the six moves are already visible.
EvidenceTrade series 2015–2025; company evidenceExhibit5. Where each product stands
3.2
Machines for factories follow Chinese factoriesWhen Chinese manufacturers build plants in Thailand, Vietnam or Indonesia, they buy the machines they know. Haitian, the largest maker of plastic-moulding machines, says so in its own results. China's share of these imports rose from 24% to 60% in ten years, and Japan's fell from 38% to 16%. Service and local plants follow fast. Trade duties follow fast too: India taxed Chinese laser cutters in 2023 and plastic-moulding machines in 2025.
EvidenceHaitian, Yizumi, Bodor; India's trade-remedy findings and dutiesExhibit6. Factory machines: China vs Japan, 2015–2025
3.3
Robots: the same path, one step behindRobots are the next machine factories buy, and they are taking the same route. China became a net exporter of robots in 2025, and in the same year overtook Japan as the main supplier in all four markets with data. India, for example, went to 34% Chinese against 30% Japanese. Chinese robot makers' own businesses abroad are still small, so the share is moving through hardware sales, local partners and installers rather than Chinese-owned networks. That is where "Get in" stands; "Win the sale" and "Stay" have not arrived yet. Warehouse robots are the exception, sold worldwide from the start, and get one paragraph.
EvidenceChina customs; import data; Estun's falling overseas share; a Thai robot joint venture; Thailand's robot-parts cluster; Fanuc's shift to the US; Geek+Exhibit7. Robots: China vs Japan by country, 2019 and 2025
3.4
Off-the-shelf equipment moves on price firstCompressors and pumps are bought through distributors, and China's share is rising mostly through low-priced, often unbranded imports. Chinese brands have little local presence yet. Incumbents are answering with cheaper second ranges: ELGi launched one in 2026 on its own separate dealer network.
EvidenceImport shares; Kaishan India's small sales; ELGi's calls; Atlas Copco's Chinese brands
3.5
The window, and our view to 2030For each kind of buyer: how far Chinese firms have got, what comes next, and how long incumbents have to act. The approved "our view by category" table moves here and gains rows for factory machines and robots. It also notes which moves do not travel, such as customer credit outside heavy equipment.

Chapter 4

What should you do about it?

The playbook has limits, and incumbents are already learning from it. Every reader has a first move, and five signs will show how fast the next products move.

4.1
Where the playbook breaksThe six approved failure modes in one table. Two show where incumbents hold: buyers return where uptime matters, and a loyal installed base holds. Four show where Chinese firms stumble: trade cases, loss-making acquisitions, dealers outgrowing parts supply, and price wars at home. The trade cases now include India's duties on factory machines.
EvidenceTata Hitachi; Thai excavators; India's duties; Efort; Neta
4.2
Incumbents are already copying itThree responses: cheaper second brands (ELGi, United Tractors, Jungheinrich), trade duties pursued by incumbents with local plants, and retreat to premium segments (Volvo, Fanuc).
EvidenceCompany statements; India's case records
4.3
Your first moveOne line for each of the six readers: global incumbents; Indian and ASEAN champions; dealers; financiers; Chinese OEMs planning their next market; policymakers. Full action lists go to the appendix.
