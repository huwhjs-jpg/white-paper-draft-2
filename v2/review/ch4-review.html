Thai excavators are often cited as a case where Japanese brands held. The record is less clear. China's share of Thai excavator imports barely moved between 2019 and 2024, but SANY's Thai distributor reports that it was already the country's top excavator seller in 2020 and 2021, with 30.4% of the market in 2021.38,N41 Appendix A sets out the detail.

Each move also carries its own risk. Credit-led sales are the most exposed in a downturn, which is why the 2026–27 slowdown matters (Chapter 1), and credit between related companies draws regulators' attention. Resale value is still unproven either way: Tata Hitachi's managing director says customers find Chinese machines have "no resale price", but that is a competitor's claim, and no free public data on used prices by brand exist to test it.42 The established brands also lead on machine data; Komatsu tracks about 840,000 machines through its Komtrax system, and no Chinese maker publishes a comparable figure.123 Local plants depend on trade policy, and an announced plant is not always a working one (Chapter 3). v1 · Chapter 13. Where the playbook breaks

In briefThe playbook breaks in six documented ways. Two show where incumbents hold; four show where Chinese entrants are exposed. Each has an early-warning sign, and each points to a reader who can act on it.

A paper about learning from Chinese OEMs would be incomplete without the cases where their approach has not worked. Each failure mode below gives what happens, the evidence, the sign to watch and the learning it limits.

Exhibit 28: Each way the playbook breaks has an early-warning sign and a reader who can act

v1 · Chapter 13. Where the playbook breaks · Where incumbents hold

1. Buyers return where uptime mattersLimits Learning 3

Where machines work hard and their resale value matters, buyers come back to incumbents. Tata Hitachi's share of Indian mining excavators fell from about 50% to 20% as Chinese makers entered, then recovered to about 35%.42

Watch for incumbent share rising in mining and other high-utilisation segments.

2. A loyal installed base holdsLimits Learning 1

A large installed base with local production and dealers can keep a category even when neighbouring categories are lost. In Thai excavators Japan's share of imports rose from 39.8% to 50.8% while China's stayed at about 36%.38

Watch for Chinese share flat in a category while it rises in the categories around it.

v1 · Chapter 13. Where the playbook breaks · Where Chinese entrants are exposed

3. High share invites trade casesLimits Learnings 2 and 3

Trade remedies follow high share, and they follow fastest where credit between related companies is hard for regulators to see through. India's regulator rejected SANY's and XCMG's submissions in the crane case and recommended duties of up to 52.03%, although the government did not impose them.14,37 Export success also triggers remedies elsewhere. The UK imposed duties of 18.81–40.08% on Chinese-made excavators until 2030; the EU has put combined duties of up to 66.7% on Chinese mobile access equipment and, in December 2025, opened an anti-dumping case on Chinese mobile cranes.43,96,125

Watch for near-total import share in a category, local producers exiting, and a petition filed.

4. Overseas acquisitions lose moneyLimits Learning 4

Buying abroad without the capability to run the business destroys value. Efort's revenue fell 32.12% in 2025; it booked a RMB154.8m impairment on its overseas system-integration business, lost RMB497m and has not made a profit in any year since 2016.44,45,157

Watch for impairments on acquired overseas units.

5. Dealer networks outgrow parts supplyLimits Learnings 1 and 2

A dealer network that grows faster than its parts supply and its parent's finances breaks quickly. After Neta's parent went bankrupt, its Thai dealers sought more than THB200m and some repairs waited up to ten months for parts (Chapter 12).46

Watch for dealer numbers rising faster than parts depots and technicians, and lengthening parts waits.

6. Price wars at home drain cashLimits all six learnings

Price wars in China weaken the capacity to invest abroad. Efort's robot revenue fell about 21.5% in 2025 and its robot gross margin about 6.5 points, which the company put down to intense price competition and its own aggressive bidding.44

Watch for falling domestic margins at the parent company.

Read together, the failure modes show where the contest is decided. Incumbents hold where a machine's value over its life matters more than its price. Entrants are exposed where they grow faster than their support, or where their share grows faster than host governments will accept. Both point to the same ground: parts, service, finance and residual value, which is where the actions in Chapter 14 concentrate.

v1 · Chapter 13. Where the playbook breaks · What remains unproven

Incumbents' claim that Chinese machines have “no resale price” remains untested in public data.42 Until a credible dataset of used-equipment prices by brand exists, it is best treated as a claim. Whichever side publishes such evidence first is likely to shape how lenders price Chinese machines.

v1 · Chapter 5. Learning 2: Treat customer credit as part of the product · Where it stops working

Credit-led share is exposed in a downturn. The 2026 coal-quota cut in Indonesia and payment delays in Indian road building will test customers who bought on easy terms (Chapter 3). Opaque credit between related companies also invites trade remedies, as India's crane case shows.14

v1 · Chapter 6. Learning 3: Price just enough, then invest in the lifecycle · Where it stops working

Resale value is unresolved. Tata Hitachi's managing director has said customers find Chinese machines have “no resale price” and higher failure rates.42 These are an incumbent's claims, and no free public dataset of used prices by brand exists to test them.

v1 · Chapter 7. Learning 4: Localise plants, then export from the host · Where it stops working

Plant economics depend on trade policy. The US investigation into excess capacity covers all five markets in this paper and could reach plants in the region (Chapter 15).65

v1 · Chapter 8. Learning 5: Turn local-content and investment rules into an advantage · Where it stops working

Rules now move in both directions. India's tender rules still keep Chinese machines out of public purchasing, and Indonesia's exemption for US goods changes the calculation only for US-brand OEMs.

The established brands are already copying it

What changed

New text

The established brands have answered in three ways, and each borrows from the Chinese approach.

Cheaper second brands. ELGi has launched a low-cost compressor range on its own separate distributor network (Chapter 3). United Tractors sells a cheaper second-tier line alongside Komatsu machines and includes up to three years of preventive maintenance with every unit.84 Jungheinrich launched AntOn, a value range of lithium-ion forklifts, in six Asian markets in January 2026; it is made for Jungheinrich by its Chinese partner EP Equipment.130,131 Atlas Copco has owned two Chinese compressor brands for two decades (Chapter 3). For these firms, learning from the Chinese playbook already means sourcing from it.

Trade defence from local plants. Established brands that make machines in India use India's trade rules. Milacron India and Shibaura Machine India were among the four applicants in the case that led to duties on Chinese plastic-moulding machines.N16 The rules cut both ways: Bystronic's China-made laser machines pay a 30.16% duty in India.N18

Retreat to premium. Volvo sold its 70% stake in SDLG, its value brand, in June 2025 to refocus on premium machines for mining, quarrying and heavy infrastructure.100 In robots, Fanuc has lost its top position in China's shipments. Fanuc is building a US$90m plant in Michigan and Yaskawa a US$180m plant in Wisconsin, and in FY2025 the two companies' combined sales in the Americas passed their sales in China.N27

Each answer has a cost. A second brand works best where buyers are led by price, as in forklifts and compressors. Trade defence buys time, but it pushes Chinese makers to build behind the wall, as HSG's Indian factory plan shows. Retreat gives up volume, and with it the parts and service revenue that a large installed base earns.

Every reader has a first move

What changed

New text

Each reader has a different stake in all this, and a different first step. Exhibit 8 sets out the actions, and Appendix C gives the full lists with time horizons and checks for choosing a partner. In brief:

  • Global incumbents: aim their finance arms at first-time and small-fleet buyers, and publish uptime and resale evidence by model before a competitor or a lender defines it.
  • Indian and ASEAN champions: use India's Press Note 2 and Indonesia's local-content rules to take Chinese technology or capital on their own terms.
  • Dealers: add a challenger brand in the niches where entrants land, with parts-supply commitments written into the contract.
  • Financiers: build residual-value data by brand and model, and test equipment loans against Indonesia's coal quotas and Indian payment delays.
  • Chinese OEMs planning their next market: expect a trade case once import share nears total, keep credit to related companies transparent, and build parts depth before adding dealers.
  • Policymakers: watch credit quality in equipment finance, including the new maker-owned finance companies, and tie incentives to exports and local suppliers.

v1 · Chapter 15. Outlook to 2030 and signposts · Signposts to watch

Exhibit 30: Six of the eight signposts are likely to move within a year

  1. India's next trade-remedy decision on Chinese capital goods. The crane duties were recommended but not imposed. A duty imposed on any Chinese capital good would signal a change of stance. Watch DGTR and the Finance Ministry.37
  2. The first Press Note 2 approvals in capital goods. They will show whether the minority joint-venture route is real.34
  3. The outcome of the US excess-capacity investigation. Tariffs that name plants in the five markets would change the export economics of Asian plants. Watch USTR and the Federal Register.65
  4. Entry into force of the EU–India trade agreement. It will lower European OEMs' costs of serving India.59,60
  5. Further finance-company licences for equipment makers in Indonesia. A second maker-owned licence would show captive finance spreading. Watch OJK.23
  6. United Tractors' and Hexindo's market shares. Komatsu back above 25% for two quarters would signal an incumbent counter-attack.81,83
  7. The electric share of Chinese equipment exports. Only 135 of 73,295 excavators exported in the first half of 2026 were electric; a share above 5% would mark the next wedge.53
  8. Indonesia's coal quotas. A 2027 quota above the 2026 level would bring mining-fleet demand back.88

We suggest reviewing the signposts twice a year, alongside the planning cycle. Signposts 5 and 6 move monthly and give the earliest read on the contest in Indonesia. Signposts 1 to 4 move with policy decisions and can change the economics of a plant at a stroke. Signposts 7 and 8 set the pace of the next technology generation and of the mining cycle.
