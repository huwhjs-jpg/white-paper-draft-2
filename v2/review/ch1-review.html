New text

Three numbers show the size of the shift. Between 2019 and 2024 China's share of Indonesia's excavator imports rose from 33% to 75%, and its share of India's crane imports from 35% to 93%.7,9 And in 2025 China overtook Japan as the largest source of industrial robots in all four markets that have reported 2025 data: India, Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia (Chapter 3).N1

The gains are broad. Between 2019 and 2024 China's share rose in 18 of the 19 country-and-product pairs we can measure, and it now exceeds half in 12 (Exhibit 1). To track progress we use one simple measure: the year China overtook Japan, long the region's main foreign supplier, as the larger source of a product's imports. For excavators it was 2019; for robots, 2025. Chapter 3 gives the year for each product.N1

These figures are import values by country of origin, as reported to UN Comtrade. They show where a machine was shipped from, not whose badge it carries, and they miss machines made locally. That matters most in India, which makes more than 95% of the construction equipment it buys.77 Appendix D sets out the limits.

v1 · Executive summary

The shift is large. Between 2019 and 2024 China's share of Indonesia's excavator imports rose from 33% to 75% while Japan's fell from 24% to 7%, and China's share of India's crane imports rose from 35% to 93%.7,9 By 2024 China supplied about two-thirds of forklift imports in India, Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia.10–13 The leading Chinese makers now earn around half or more of their revenue abroad; SANY earns 63%.1

v1 · Chapter 2. Where the shift is happening

In briefChinese gains are broad but uneven. China supplies almost all of India's crane imports, three-quarters of Indonesia's excavator imports and about two-thirds of forklift imports across the region, but it has made no headway in Thai excavators.

This chapter uses the one consistent public dataset across all five countries: the value of imports by country of origin, as reported to UN Comtrade. Import data record where a machine was shipped from, not whose badge it carries, and they miss machines built locally. Appendix A sets out these limits.

v1 · Chapter 2. Where the shift is happening · China's share of imports rose almost everywhere

Between 2019 and 2024, China's share of import value rose in 18 of the 19 country–category pairs with data, and it now exceeds half in 12. Three patterns stand out.

Exhibit 7: China's share of import value by country and category, 2019 → 2024 (cell colour shows the 2024 share)

  • Forklifts are China's most consistent gain. China supplies about two-thirds or more of forklift imports in every country with 2024 data, up from 32–53% in 2019. The switch to electric forklifts explains much of this (Chapter 9).
  • Cranes show the starkest change. China's share of India's self-propelled crane imports rose from 35% to 93% while their value more than quadrupled, to US$623m.9 Chapter 10 examines how.
  • Excavators split the region. China now supplies three-quarters of Indonesia's excavator imports, half of Malaysia's and a third of India's, but its share of Thai imports barely moved (Chapter 12).

In compressors and pumps China was already the largest supplier in most of these markets in 2019, and it gained further; in Thai compressors it overtook Japan.68 The one fall is in Indian pumps, where China's share was flat at about 35%, consistent with a strong domestic pump industry.70

Indian figures need a caution. More than 95% of the construction equipment sold in India is made there, so import shares describe a small slice of the market.77 They also miss the machines Chinese makers build in their Indian plants (Chapter 7), and so understate the Chinese presence.

New text

Chinese makers went abroad in three stages (Exhibit 2).

  • Exporting the surplus, to 2021. China's home market was booming, and exports were a sideline: 7–11% of excavator unit sales each year from 2016 to 2020, while home sales grew from 62,993 to a record 292,864.N2–N4,49
  • Going overseas-first, 2022–24. Then the home market collapsed. Domestic excavator sales fell by almost 70% between 2020 and 2023, to 89,980, while exports tripled to 105,038.49,50 The turn came first in company results. In 2022 SANY's overseas revenue rose from 23% to 45.7% of its total, and its gross margin abroad, 26.4%, was higher than at home.N5 In 2023 the industry sold more excavators abroad than at home.50 By 2025 SANY earned 63% of its revenue outside China.1
  • Owning the system, from 2025. Chinese makers began to own what surrounds the machine abroad: plants, finance companies, repair and parts. Since November 2025 they have opened or broken ground on five plants and service bases in Indonesia and Thailand. In September 2026 Indonesia licensed SANY's own finance company, and XCMG broke ground on its first overseas base for repairing and rebuilding mining-machine parts.23,24,29–32 Chapter 2 describes these moves.

The rules changed in the same window, and mostly towards local production. India dropped a planned certification rule for machinery in January 2026 and in March opened a 60-day decision track for minority Chinese investment in capital goods. The duties its trade regulator recommended on Chinese cranes in September 2025 were not imposed within the three months its rules allow.57,34,37 Indonesia now counts investment in a local plant towards the local-content score that governs public purchasing.33 Since 24 July 2026 the United States has charged a 10% tariff on goods from India, Indonesia and Malaysia under a trade action over forced labour, against 12.5% on goods from China, Thailand and Vietnam.67

The newest Chinese moves are about what surrounds the machine. v1 · Chapter 1. Why learn from Chinese OEMs now · The rules reset

At least nine policy events in 2026 changed how equipment is made, sold and financed in the region. Together they push towards local production and away from exporting from China.

Exhibit 6: Six policy events in 2026 opened access and three raised barriers

  • India made the largest moves. In January it withdrew a planned certification rule for pumps, compressors, cranes and construction equipment.57 In March, Press Note 2 created a 60-day decision track for minority investment in capital goods from China and other countries that share a land border with India (Chapter 8).34,35 India also held back.   • Trade agreements changed access. 1.3

It is not a commodity boom

What changed

New text

Did Chinese makers simply ride a mining boom? The evidence says no, for two reasons.

First, incumbents lost share while their own market grew. In Indonesia, Komatsu's share of heavy-equipment sales, as reported by its distributor United Tractors, fell from 29% in 2023 to 20% in 2025. Over the same years Indonesia mined record amounts of coal: 836 million tonnes in 2024 and 817 million in 2025.15,17,18,N6 Dividing Komatsu's sales by its share gives a rough market size: on that basis Komatsu sold about 750 fewer machines in a market that we estimate grew by about 4,400 units. Hitachi's share, as reported by its distributor Hexindo, followed a similar path.82,83

Second, China gained where mining plays no part: in forklifts, cranes, compressors and pumps, and in the machines factories buy (Chapter 3).11,72–74,N1 Thailand has no coal or nickel boom, yet China's import share rose there in four of the five main categories. The exception is excavators, where China's share barely moved between 2019 and 2024.12,38,39,68,69

On the five tests in Appendix D, our verdict is that the shift is structural in India and Indonesia, and in four of five categories in Thailand. Commodity cycles amplified it but did not cause it. Indonesia's nickel boom brought new, often Chinese-owned customers, but it cannot explain Chinese gains in forklifts or pumps.30,84

The market is still contestable where machines work hardest. In Indian mining excavators, Tata Hitachi lost share to Chinese entrants and then won much of it back (Chapter 4).42

We tested whether this is a commodity cycle, and it is not. v1 · Chapter 3. Structural or cyclical?

In briefCoal, nickel and highway cycles amplified Chinese gains but did not cause them. In India and Indonesia the shift passes the first three tests outright, holds in a control market for four of five categories, and remains contestable where uptime and resale value decide the purchase.

A sceptical reader will ask whether Komatsu lost share in Indonesia because Chinese firms had a better commercial system, or because coal miners, Komatsu's most important customers, stopped buying. The answer decides strategy. A cyclical loss can be waited out; a structural one cannot.

v1 · Chapter 3. Structural or cyclical? · Five tests

We apply five tests. The first asks whether the incumbent loses share in good years as well as bad, the second whether the shift began before the cyclical shock, and the third whether it appears outside the cyclical segment, which here means mining. A yes on any of these points to a structural shift. The fourth compares a similar market without the cycle: a yes supports a structural reading, and a no points to an effect specific to the category. The fifth asks whether lost share has come back anywhere; a yes means the market is still contestable. We then grade each country on a five-point scale: structural, likely structural, inconclusive, likely cyclical or cyclical.

v1 · Chapter 3. Structural or cyclical? · Indonesia: share fell

  • Test 1: good years. Komatsu's share fell from 29% to 20% between 2023 and 2025, while Indonesia mined a record 836 million tonnes of coal in 2024 and 817 million in 2025, and the market we estimate grew by about a third.15,17–19 Hexindo's figures point the same way: its unit sales rose 22% while its share barely moved.83
  • Test 2: timing. China's share of excavator imports rose from 33% to 75% between 2019 and 2024, before the 2026 cut in coal production quotas, which the energy ministry expects to reduce output by 12%, to about 720 million tonnes.7,88 The cut does explain the latest fall: in January to August 2026, United Tractors' Komatsu sales fell 21% to 2,689 units, and its sales of large mining machines fell 49%.89,90
  • Test 3: outside mining. China's share also rose in forklifts, cranes, compressors and pumps, none of which depends mainly on mining.11,72–74 Hexindo, whose largest segment is agribusiness, lost share too.83
  • Test 4: control market. Thailand has no coal or nickel boom, yet China gained there in four of five categories. It did not gain in excavators (Chapter 12).
  • Test 5: reversal. Share has come back before. Komatsu's rose from 21% in 2021 to 29% in 2023, and Hitachi's from 15.8% to 21%.15,79,82

Nickel is often offered as the explanation. Indonesia's policy of processing nickel at home created a new group of customers, many of them Chinese-owned, and SANY's gains have been linked to them.84 The first electric loaders from XCMG's Weda Bay plant went to Tsingshan.30 The effect is real, but it cannot explain Chinese gains in forklifts, pumps or plantation machinery. Nickel amplified the shift; it did not cause it.

v1 · Chapter 3. Structural or cyclical? · India: Chinese gains

  • Test 1: good years. Construction-equipment sales rose 26% in FY24 and reached 140,191 units in FY25, before slipping 2% in FY26.20,93 Over those years China's share of excavator imports rose from 7.7% to 33.9%, and Chinese crane imports from 163 to 938 units a year.8,14
  • Test 2: timing. The FY26 slowdown has clear cyclical causes: highway construction slowed from 36.8 km a day in FY21 to 25 km a day in FY26, and new highway awards fell by almost half from their FY22 peak.94 The import shift began well before it.
  • Test 3: outside the cycle. China's share also rose in forklifts, from 32.5% to 67.6%, and in compressors, from 44.0% to 52.6%.10,71
  • Test 4: control market. Thailand confirms the pattern in four categories of five, as it does for Indonesia.
  • Test 5: reversal. Tata Hitachi's share of mining excavators fell from about 50% to 20% as Chinese makers entered, and has since recovered to about 35%.42

v1 · Chapter 3. Structural or cyclical? · What about state support?

State support is part of the picture. India's trade regulator calculated dumping margins of 30% to 110% on Chinese cranes, and the EU has imposed anti-subsidy duties on Chinese mobile access equipment.14,96 The collapse of China's home market also left the industry with capacity to fill.50 Subsidies and spare capacity explain why Chinese firms could afford to push abroad. They do not explain how they won customers once there. The moves set out in Part II are commercial choices that other firms can observe and, in most cases, copy.

1.4

The open question: will share bought partly on credit survive the downturn?

What changed

  • New section, built from the v1 downturn paragraph and Learning 2's limit.
  • Coal updated from the top-up: quotas first cut to about 600 Mt, slow work-plan approvals, lenders waiting on approval, and the ministry's projection of about 720 Mt (a projection, not a quota).
  • Highway pace: 25 km a day in FY26 kept; 21 km a day now labelled as the FY27 expectation.
  • The approved central question closes the chapter, as the benchmark checklist asks, with a pointer to Chapters 2–4. "OEM" is defined here once.

New text

Some of the share was won with easy credit (Chapter 2). The 2026–27 downturn in both main markets will test whether it holds.

  • Chinese makers are still pushing. China's excavator exports rose 33.5% in the first half of 2026.53
  • Indonesia's miners are buying less. The government first cut 2026 coal quotas to about 600 million tonnes, and approvals of miners' annual work plans (RKAB) came slowly. Lenders held back finance until a customer's plan was approved.N7,N8 With revisions largely approved by late September, the energy ministry expects output of about 720 million tonnes, down 12% on 2025.88,N9 United Tractors, Komatsu's distributor, does not expect demand to recover until 2027.97
  • India's road builders are slowing and waiting to be paid. Highway construction slowed from 36.8 km a day in FY21 to 25 km a day in FY26, with about 21 km a day expected in FY27. The manufacturers' association points to payment and project delays.94,95

Buyers on easy terms are the most exposed to delays like these. If Chinese share holds, credit will have been a tool rather than a crutch. Chapter 4 names the signs to watch.

That leaves the question this paper answers. Which of the moves that won Chinese OEMs share can be copied, by whom and how fast, and which product categories will they reach next? (An OEM, or original equipment maker, is the company whose brand the machine carries.) Chapter 2 answers which moves, Chapter 3 which products come next, and Chapter 4 by whom and how fast.

v1 · Chapter 3. Structural or cyclical? · Verdict

The downturn now under way in both main markets, driven by coal quotas in Indonesia and highway awards in India, will depress 2026 unit sales for every brand. United Tractors does not expect demand to start recovering until 2027.97 The downturn will also test the parts of the Chinese model that depend on credit, because buyers who bought on easy finance are the most exposed to project delays and payment backlogs.

v1 · Chapter 5. Learning 2: Treat customer credit as part of the product · Where it stops working

Credit-led share is exposed in a downturn. The 2026 coal-quota cut in Indonesia and payment delays in Indian road building will test customers who bought on easy terms (Chapter 3). Opaque credit between related companies also invites trade remedies, as India's crane case shows.14

v1 · Chapter 1. Why learn from Chinese OEMs now · The question for this paper

For firms now setting their 2027–2030 plans, three planning assumptions follow. Chinese competitors treat overseas growth as a necessity. They are building the finance and aftermarket layers where incumbents earn their margins. And policy in India and Indonesia is moving from keeping Chinese firms out to channelling Chinese capital into local production.

The question for industrial leaders is therefore no longer whether Chinese OEMs are serious competitors in India and Southeast Asia. It is which of the moves that won them share can be copied, by whom, and how fast. Chapter 2 maps where share has moved, Chapter 3 tests whether the shift will last, and Chapters 4 to 9 set out the six moves.
