Chapter 1. What happened?

In briefIn under a decade Chinese makers became the main suppliers of heavy equipment in India and Southeast Asia. The shift turned twice, in 2022 and 2025, and it is structural, not a commodity boom.

Chinese makers now supply most of the region's imported heavy equipment

Three numbers show the size of the shift. Between 2019 and 2024 China's share of Indonesia's excavator imports rose from 33% to 75%, and its share of India's crane imports from 35% to 93%.7,9 And in 2025 China overtook Japan as the largest source of industrial robots in all four markets that have reported 2025 data: India, Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia (Chapter 3).N1

The gains are broad. Between 2019 and 2024 China's share rose in 18 of the 19 country-and-product pairs we can measure, and it now exceeds half in 12 (Exhibit 1). China supplies about two-thirds or more of forklift imports in every country with 2024 data. Only excavators split the region: China supplies three-quarters of Indonesia's imports and half of Malaysia's, but its share in Thailand barely moved.7,10–13,38,40

To track progress we use one simple measure: the year China overtook Japan, long the region's main foreign supplier, as the larger source of a product's imports. For excavators it was 2019; for robots, 2025. Chapter 3 gives the year for each product.N1

These figures are import values by country of origin, as reported to UN Comtrade. They show where a machine was shipped from, not whose badge it carries, and they miss machines made locally. That matters most in India, which makes more than 95% of the construction equipment it buys.77 Appendix D sets out the limits.

The shift turned twice: overseas-first in 2022, owning the system from 2025

Chinese makers went abroad in three stages (Exhibit 2).

  • Exporting the surplus, to 2021. China's home market was booming, and exports were a sideline: 7–11% of excavator unit sales each year from 2016 to 2020, while home sales grew from 62,993 to a record 292,864.N2–N4,49
  • Going overseas-first, 2022–24. Then the home market collapsed. Domestic excavator sales fell by almost 70% between 2020 and 2023, to 89,980, while exports tripled to 105,038.49,50 The turn came first in company results. In 2022 SANY's overseas revenue rose from 23% to 45.7% of its total, and its gross margin abroad, 26.4%, was higher than at home.N5 In 2023 the industry sold more excavators abroad than at home.50 By 2025 SANY earned 63% of its revenue outside China.1
  • Owning the system, from 2025. Chinese makers began to own what surrounds the machine abroad: plants, finance companies, repair and parts. Since November 2025 they have opened or broken ground on five plants and service bases in Indonesia and Thailand. In September 2026 Indonesia licensed SANY's own finance company, and XCMG broke ground on its first overseas base for repairing and rebuilding mining-machine parts.23,24,29–32 Chapter 2 describes these moves.

The rules changed in the same window, and mostly towards local production. India dropped a planned certification rule for machinery in January 2026 and in March opened a 60-day decision track for minority Chinese investment in capital goods. The duties its trade regulator recommended on Chinese cranes in September 2025 were not imposed within the three months its rules allow.57,34,37 Indonesia now counts investment in a local plant towards the local-content score that governs public purchasing.33 Since 24 July 2026 the United States has charged a 10% tariff on goods from India, Indonesia and Malaysia under a trade action over forced labour, against 12.5% on goods from China, Thailand and Vietnam.67

It is not a commodity boom

Did Chinese makers simply ride a mining boom? The evidence says no, for two reasons.

First, incumbents lost share while their own market grew. In Indonesia, Komatsu's share of heavy-equipment sales, as reported by its distributor United Tractors, fell from 29% in 2023 to 20% in 2025. Over the same years Indonesia mined record amounts of coal: 836 million tonnes in 2024 and 817 million in 2025.15,17,18,N6 Dividing Komatsu's sales by its share gives a rough market size: on that basis Komatsu sold about 750 fewer machines in a market that we estimate grew by about 4,400 units. Hitachi's share, as reported by its distributor Hexindo, followed a similar path.82,83

Second, China gained where mining plays no part: in forklifts, cranes, compressors and pumps, and in the machines factories buy (Chapter 3).11,72–74,N1 Thailand has no coal or nickel boom, yet China's import share rose there in four of the five main categories. The exception is excavators, where China's share barely moved between 2019 and 2024.12,38,39,68,69

On the five tests in Appendix D, our verdict is that the shift is structural in India and Indonesia, and in four of five categories in Thailand. Commodity cycles amplified it but did not cause it. Indonesia's nickel boom brought new, often Chinese-owned customers, but it cannot explain Chinese gains in forklifts or pumps.30,84

The market is still contestable where machines work hardest. In Indian mining excavators, Tata Hitachi lost share to Chinese entrants and then won much of it back (Chapter 4).42

The open question: will share bought partly on credit survive the downturn?

Some of the share was won with easy credit (Chapter 2). The 2026–27 downturn in both main markets will test whether it holds.

  • Chinese makers are still pushing. China's excavator exports rose 33.5% in the first half of 2026.53
  • Indonesia's miners are buying less. The government first cut 2026 coal quotas to about 600 million tonnes, and approvals of miners' annual work plans (RKAB) came slowly. Lenders held back finance until a customer's plan was approved.N7,N8 With revisions largely approved by late September, the energy ministry expects output of about 720 million tonnes, down 12% on 2025.88,N9 United Tractors, Komatsu's distributor, does not expect demand to recover until 2027.97
  • India's road builders are slowing and waiting to be paid. Highway construction slowed from 36.8 km a day in FY21 to 25 km a day in FY26, with about 21 km a day expected in FY27. The manufacturers' association points to payment and project delays.94,95

Buyers on easy terms are the most exposed to delays like these. If Chinese share holds, credit will have been a tool rather than a crutch. Chapter 4 names the signs to watch.

That leaves the question this paper answers. Which of the moves that won Chinese OEMs share can be copied, by whom and how fast, and which product categories will they reach next? (An OEM, or original equipment maker, is the company whose brand the machine carries.) Chapter 2 answers which moves, Chapter 3 which products come next, and Chapter 4 by whom and how fast.
