Chapter 2. How did they do it?

In briefNot by being cheapest. Chinese makers redesigned how the machine is sold, financed, serviced and made. Each move can be copied, and most are cheaper for incumbents to copy than they were for the Chinese firms that made them first.

Six moves explain most of the gains. They come in three pairs, and the easiest way to see them is to follow the companies: how they got into a market, how they won customers once there, and how they made their position last. Where each move works less well is the subject of Chapter 4.

Getting in: start where the big brands are thin

Chinese makers rarely attacked the established brands head on. They came in where those brands were thin.

SANY's start in India shows the first way in: enter through a narrow beachhead, then broaden. SANY began building in India at Chakan, near Pune, but it did not start in the core of the excavator market. It went after long-reach excavators, a specialist machine used to clean canals, dig wells and build metro lines. Within about 18 months it had 52% of that niche nationally and 62% in Maharashtra, figures it announced in October 2016.21

The niche mattered less for its size than for what it forced SANY to build: dealers, service engineers and parts depots. That network could then sell everything else. By 2025 SANY India claimed 60% of India's piling rigs, 55% of its cranes, 50% of its port machinery and 20% of its excavators. These are company claims, but trade data and India's crane case support their direction.22 By September 2026 it reported about 42 dealers and more than 45,000 machines delivered across India and South Asia.98 In Indonesia the opening was a group of customers rather than a product: the companies building nickel smelters and mines, many of them Chinese-owned.82,84 Following Chinese customers abroad has since become the main route into the machines factories buy, as Chapter 3 shows.

The second way in was to use electrification as the entry wedge. A change of technology is the moment when buyers are most willing to try a new brand. Chinese forklift makers arrived just as buyers moved to electric machines, and in 2024 China supplied 60–69% of electric-forklift imports in India, Indonesia and Thailand. In Thailand Japan's share fell to 13.6%.10–12 The Chinese makers' own sales lead with electric: 70.15% of Heli's 2025 unit sales were electric.36 Cheap batteries help. BloombergNEF put the average battery pack at US$108 per kWh in 2025, and packs made in China at US$84.132 Construction equipment is only at the start of the same change, but Chinese makers are already positioning for it: XCMG's Weda Bay plant in Indonesia makes electric loaders, LiuGong's new Karawang plant will make only electric machines, and SANY India launched an electric excavator and loader in 2026.29,30,98

What others can copy. Choose a niche for the dealer network it builds, not only for its size. Established brands should look hard at the segments where their own range is thin, because that is where the next entrant will land. In construction equipment the move to electric has barely begun, so the next generation of machines is the moment to win customers, with battery supply secured early.

Winning customers: finance the buyer, then earn on the machine's life

Many equipment buyers in India and Indonesia are small contractors and miners who struggle to borrow from banks. For them the down payment and the monthly instalment matter more than the list price. Tata Hitachi's managing director said in September 2026 that first-time buyers in road construction were turning to non-bank lenders, and that Chinese makers had gained ground with easier financing.42 So Chinese makers learned to treat customer credit as part of the product.

SANY's path in Indonesia shows the move in full (Exhibit 3). In December 2017 its official distributor, PT Sany Perkasa, signed a memorandum of understanding with MNC Leasing on equipment finance, and a year later the two launched low-down-payment loans.92,103,104 In September 2025 SANY signed an agreement worth about RMB1.45bn with two finance companies.106 On 14 September 2026 Indonesia's financial regulator licensed SANY's own finance company, PT Sany Indonesia Finance.23 In the same month XCMG and its dealer launched ABC Multifinance, a joint venture that finances a machine across its purchase, maintenance, use and resale.56,110 Local lenders now treat Chinese brands as normal credit risk: one of SANY's 2025 finance partners belongs to the group of Trakindo, Indonesia's Caterpillar dealer.108,109

Credit also flows between related companies. In India's crane case the trade regulator found that XCMG's exporter gave its related Indian importer credit of up to three years, which it called "not normal", and that XCMG had misreported the terms. The regulator took the credit into account when it calculated export prices.14

Price played a smaller part than most people assume. The approach was to price just enough, then invest in the lifecycle. The only regulator-checked comparison in these markets found that Chinese cranes undercut Indian prices by just 0–10% between April 2023 and March 2024. What hurt the Indian producer was a squeeze, with Chinese landed prices below its cost of sales.14 Asking prices point the same way. On Indonesia's public e-catalogue a SANY 21-tonne excavator was listed at Rp2.10bn, against Rp2.25bn for Komatsu's equivalent, both including tax.N40 The gap was wider earlier: in 2021, as SANY's Indonesian share reached 21% against Komatsu's 22%, it had cut prices by about 20%.99 The pattern is a gap large enough to matter, but not so large that buyers see a different class of machine.

The money then goes into the machine's working life: parts, servicing and rebuilding. This is where the established brands have always been strongest. In the first half of 2026 United Tractors' heavy-equipment revenue fell 28%, yet its revenue from spare parts and maintenance rose 1% to Rp5.5 trillion, about 37% of the total. It supports that business with 40 workshops and remanufacturing sites and 117 warehouses.89,118 Chinese makers are now building the same. On 16 September 2026 XCMG broke ground in Balikpapan on its first overseas base for overhauling and remanufacturing mining-machine parts, and its aftermarket revenue grew 33.23% in the first half of 2025, four times as fast as the company as a whole.24,119 XCMG's Indonesian service policy promises a response within 15 minutes and parts within 72 hours, and SANY India lists more than 120 service branches.120,121

What others can copy. Established brands with their own finance arms can copy the credit move most cheaply: design the down payment, loan term, seasonal repayments and trade-in terms together with the machine, and aim them at first-time buyers. Hitachi's Indonesian distributor already lists its own financing as one answer to Chinese competition, but there is little public sign that the big brands are aiming finance at the small buyers Chinese makers have won.82 On the machine's working life, they should treat parts, service and rebuilding as a business to grow, and publish evidence on uptime and resale value before a competitor or a lender does it for them.

Staying: build locally and use the rules

Two moves make a position hard to dislodge. The first is to localise plants, then export from the host. Four Chinese makers build in India. SANY has invested more than INR 1,000 crore in its plant at Chakan, and SANY India shipped about 1,200 machines abroad in 2024. XCMG has made machines in Chennai since 2021, Zoomlion opened an INR 500 crore plant at Khed City in May 2024, and LiuGong opened an INR 272 crore expansion at Pithampur in July 2026.22,25–27,98

Since November 2025 Chinese makers have opened or broken ground on five plants and service bases in Indonesia and Thailand (Exhibit 4):

  • Heli's forklift and battery joint venture in Rayong, Thailand (November 2025), planned for 10,000 forklifts and 10,000 battery sets a year;32
  • Hangcha's plant in Chonburi, Thailand (opened June 2026);31
  • LiuGong's US$317m plant at Karawang, Indonesia, for 5,000 electric machines a year (July 2026);28,29
  • XCMG's Weda Bay plant, "XCMG's first overseas new-energy factory", which began making electric loaders in July 2026;30
  • XCMG's remanufacturing base in Balikpapan (September 2026).24

Three reasons recur. A local plant opens government purchasing under local-content rules. It insures against tariffs and trade cases aimed at Chinese-made goods, such as the UK's duties of 18.81–40.08% on Chinese excavators.43 And it puts the maker next to its customers: XCMG's Weda Bay plant sits in the industrial park where its first customer, Tsingshan, operates.30 In time the host becomes an export base. LiuGong calls Karawang its production base for the Asia-Pacific before it expands exports to North America and Europe.28,29

The second is to turn local-content and investment rules into an advantage. Indonesia's revised rules give a product a 25% credit towards its local-content score for investing in a factory in Indonesia, with further credits for local workers and good management. Where a domestic product's combined score exceeds 40%, government buyers must use it and may not import.33 The established brands hold the advantage today. Komatsu's 20-tonne excavator scores 51.2% on the public e-catalogue (local content plus a weighting for the company's local benefits), while SANY's equivalent shows 0% and is listed as an import.N40 Chinese makers will need local production to close the gap, which is one reason LiuGong has committed to raising its local content at Karawang.29

India has moved from blocking Chinese capital to channelling it. Since 2020, investment from countries that share a land border with India has needed government approval. Press Note 2, approved in March 2026, eases this for minority stakes: proposals in capital goods and some related sectors are to be decided within 60 days, provided majority ownership and control stay with resident Indians.34 Legal commentators note that the 60-day timeline may not be binding.35 Other rules still point the other way. Since July 2020, firms from those countries may bid in government tenders only after registering with a government committee.126 The result is that Chinese capital can now enter India as a minority partner more easily than Chinese-made machines can win government tenders.

What others can copy. Most established brands already own plants in the region; Volvo, for example, will launch wheel loaders built in India in 2027.101 The open question is whether they use those plants as export hubs as deliberately as Chinese entrants plan to. Plant decisions should be judged on export value, tariff protection and access to government purchasing, not home demand alone. Local-content thresholds are better treated as a route into government purchasing than as a compliance cost, because the first to qualify often sets the specification. Indian and ASEAN companies can use Press Note 2 to take Chinese technology and minority capital while keeping control.

None of these six moves depends on subsidies. They are commercial choices, and most are cheaper for the established brands, which already own finance arms, large customer bases and local plants, than they were for the Chinese firms that made them first. The next question is where the same moves go next.
