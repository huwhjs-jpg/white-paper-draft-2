About this paper

This paper treats Chinese equipment makers as a case study in how challengers win share in emerging Asian markets, and asks which of their moves other firms can copy. We take this view because the moves that worked are commercial choices open to any firm, several are cheaper for established companies than they were for the Chinese firms that made them first, and India's 2026 investment rules make partnership with Chinese capital a live option.

What it covers

Five markets: India and Indonesia in depth, Thailand as a focused case, and Vietnam and Malaysia as comparators. Products are grouped by who buys them: contractors and fleets buying through dealers (excavators, cranes, forklifts); factories (plastic-moulding machines, machine tools, laser cutters and robots); and plant engineers buying standard equipment through distributors (compressors, pumps, generators).

How we built it

We used only free, publicly verifiable sources: UN Comtrade trade data, company filings and releases, regulators' and governments' records, and reputable media. We used no paid databases, surveys or interviews. Figures are as of 2 October 2026 unless dated otherwise, and company claims, competitors' claims and our own calculations are labelled as such; Appendix D sets out the method and its limits.

How it builds on our earlier work

Our December 2025 paper, Strategic Pathways for Industrial MNCs in Emerging Asian Growth Corridors, asked where to play in India and the ASEAN-5. This paper asks how a group of challengers won once they were there. It also extends our June 2026 paper, Global Operations in an Era of Structural Recalibration, which described Chinese outward investment in upstream industries and resource security, to market entry by Chinese equipment makers.
