Executive summary

Chinese OEMs won share mainly by redesigning the commercial system around the machine, not by selling it cheaply, and most of their moves can be copied. Several are cheaper for established companies to make than they were for the Chinese firms that made them first.

The shift is large. Between 2019 and 2024 China's share of Indonesia's excavator imports rose from 33% to 75%, and its share of India's crane imports from 35% to 93%.7,9 In 2025 China overtook Japan as the largest source of industrial robots in India, Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia.N1

It is not a commodity boom. In Indonesia, Komatsu's share of heavy-equipment sales fell from 29% to 20% between 2023 and 2025, while coal output stood near its record.15,17,18,N6 China also gained in products that have nothing to do with mining, and in Thailand, which had no boom. By our tests the shift is structural in India and Indonesia, and in four of five product categories in Thailand.

Six moves explain most of the gains, in three pairs:

  • Getting in. Enter through a narrow beachhead, then broaden: SANY took 52% of India's long-reach excavator niche within about 18 months, then sold cranes and piling rigs through the same dealers.21,22 Use electrification as the entry wedge: China supplied 60–69% of electric-forklift imports in India, Indonesia and Thailand in 2024.10–12
  • Winning customers. Treat customer credit as part of the product: SANY went from a memorandum with a lender in 2017 to its own licensed finance company in Indonesia in September 2026.23,103 Price just enough, then invest in the lifecycle: Chinese cranes undercut Indian prices by only 0–10%, and Chinese makers are now building the parts and rebuilding business that earned United Tractors 37% of its machinery revenue in the first half of 2026.14,24,89
  • Staying. Localise plants, then export from the host: four Chinese makers build in India, and five Chinese plants and service bases have opened or broken ground in Indonesia and Thailand since November 2025.24–32 Turn local-content and investment rules into an advantage: Indonesia now gives a 25% local-content credit for investing locally, and India promises a 60-day decision on minority Chinese investment in capital goods.33,34

The same moves are now reaching other products, in a different order, and the buyer decides the order. Factory machines follow Chinese factories abroad: China's share of plastic-moulding machine imports rose from 24% to 60% in ten years, and India imposed duties within a few years. Robots are on the same path a few years behind. Compressors and pumps move more slowly, on price.N1,N10,N16–N18

The approach has limits. Buyers return where reliability matters, loyal customer bases hold, and high share invites trade cases. Established brands are already copying the moves, with cheaper second ranges, trade defence from local plants and retreat to premium segments.

What this means for each group of readers:

  • Global incumbents already own the finance arms, customer bases and local plants Chinese firms spent a decade building. The priority is to aim them at first-time buyers and publish evidence on uptime and resale value.
  • Indian and ASEAN champions can use India's Press Note 2 and Indonesia's local-content rules to take Chinese technology or capital on their own terms.
  • Dealers should choose brands niche by niche and compete on parts depth and service, where Chinese networks are still thinner.
  • Financiers now hold Chinese-brand credit as a mainstream exposure and need resale-value evidence by brand to price it.
  • Chinese OEMs planning their next market should expect trade cases once their share is high, and build parts supply before adding dealers.
  • Policymakers in India and Indonesia are choosing between Chinese capital that builds locally and Chinese machines that arrive as imports. Their current rules lean towards capital.
