## What the paper is about

Over the past ten years, Chinese companies that make industrial equipment have become the main suppliers of that equipment in India and Southeast Asia. They now sell most of the imported excavators, cranes and forklifts in these countries, and they are moving fast into factory machinery and robots. This paper explains how they did it and what other companies can learn from it. It is written for business readers, not industry specialists.

**Countries:** India and Indonesia in depth, Thailand in some detail, Vietnam and Malaysia briefly.

**Products:**
- construction equipment and forklifts;
- the machines factories use (plastic-moulding machines, machine tools, laser cutters) and robots;
- everyday plant equipment (compressors, pumps, generators).

**Sources:** free public sources only.

## Locked items (unchanged from v3 and v5)

- **Main point:** Chinese OEMs won share mainly by redesigning the commercial system around the machine, not by selling it cheaply, and most of their moves can be copied.
- **Question:** Which of the moves that won Chinese OEMs share can be copied, by whom and how fast, and which product categories will they reach next?
- **Chapter 3 finding:** in the next products the same moves arrive in a different order, and the buyer decides the order.
- **Six lessons, with their approved names, in three pairs:**
  - **Getting in:** "Enter through a narrow beachhead, then broaden"; "Use electrification as the entry wedge".
  - **Winning customers:** "Treat customer credit as part of the product"; "Price just enough, then invest in the lifecycle".
  - **Staying:** "Localise plants, then export from the host"; "Turn local-content and investment rules into an advantage".
  - The numbers 1–6 are used only in lists such as the appendices, not in the running text.
- **Six readers:** global incumbents; Indian and ASEAN champions; dealers; financiers; Chinese OEMs planning their next market; policymakers.
- **Six warning signs (failure modes):**
  - buyers return where uptime matters;
  - a loyal installed base holds;
  - high share invites trade cases;
  - overseas acquisitions lose money;
  - dealer networks outgrow parts supply;
  - price wars at home drain cash.

### Chapter 1. What happened? (drafted)
- **Opens with a puzzle:** in 2025 Indonesia mined near-record coal, yet Komatsu's share fell from 29% to 20%, and most of the loss went to Chinese brands.
- **1.1 The size of the shift.** The three numbers. China's share rose in 18 of 19 country-and-product pairs; forklifts are about two-thirds Chinese; excavators split the region. Import data have limits.
  - *Exhibit 1:* heat map of China's import share.
- **1.2 The two turning points.** The three-stage timeline:
  - The 2022 turn: China's home market fell almost 70%, and SANY went from 23% to 45.7% of revenue abroad.
  - The 2025 turn: Chinese makers began owning the system abroad, marked by SANY's finance licence in September 2026.
  - One paragraph on the 2026 rule changes.
  - *Exhibit 2:* timeline.
- **1.3 Not a commodity boom.**
  - The Komatsu numbers: about 750 fewer machines in a market about 4,400 larger.
  - China also gained in non-mining products and in Thailand.
  - **Verdict:** structural in India, Indonesia and four of five Thai categories.
- **1.4 The open question.** Will share bought partly on easy credit survive the 2026–27 slowdown? Covers Indonesia's coal quotas and lenders, and India's road slowdown and late payments. The chapter ends with the paper's question and hands over to Chapter 2.

### Chapter 2. How did they do it? (drafted)
Told as three stories that follow the companies, with the lesson names in bold. Each story ends with one "What others can copy" note.
- **2.1 Getting in.**
  - SANY's long-reach excavator niche in India (52% in about 18 months), then cranes and more through the same dealers.
  - Nickel customers in Indonesia.
  - Electric forklifts: China supplied 60–69% of imports.
  - Construction is only starting to go electric.
- **2.2 Winning customers.**
  - SANY's credit path in Indonesia: 2017 MoU, 2018 low-deposit loans, 2025 finance deal, 2026 licence.
  - XCMG's finance joint venture.
  - The XCMG credit finding in India's crane case.
  - Price: cranes only 0–10% cheaper; Indonesia's e-catalogue about 7% apart.
  - Then the money goes into parts and repair (United Tractors' 37%, XCMG's Balikpapan rebuild base).
  - *Exhibit 3:* SANY's finance story.
- **2.3 Staying.**
  - Four Chinese makers build in India, and five new sites have opened or started in Indonesia and Thailand since November 2025.
  - Why build locally: government purchasing, tariff insurance, closeness to customers, and export bases.
  - Indonesia's local-content credit; India's Press Note 2.
  - *Exhibit 4:* the moves over time.
  - Ends: the moves are commercial choices, mostly cheaper for incumbents.

### Chapter 3. Where is it heading next? (drafted)
- **3.1 How a product is bought sets the pace.** Introduces the three kinds of buyer and the "overtook Japan" years, then states the Chapter 3 finding.
  - *Exhibit 5:* where each product stands.
- **3.2 Factory machines follow Chinese factories.**
  - Plastic-moulding machines rose from 24% to 60% Chinese.
  - Haitian credits "the global expansion of Chinese enterprises".
  - Service and plants followed in India, with the Haitian caution from India's trade regulator.
  - India's duties: lasers 2023, when China already supplied 83% of Indian demand; plastic-moulding machines 2025.
  - Incumbents among the applicants for the duties.
  - *Exhibit 6.*
- **3.3 Robots, a few years behind.**
  - China became a net exporter in 2025 and overtook Japan in all four markets.
  - Indonesia's jump was lumpy; without it, China rose from 21% to 37% and Japan fell from 35% to 31%.
  - Chinese robot makers' own businesses abroad are small, so robots come through partners.
  - Warehouse robots are the exception (box).
  - *Exhibit 7.*
- **3.4 Standard equipment moves on price.** Compressors and pumps are mostly unbranded imports. ELGi launched a cheaper range on separate dealers, and Atlas Copco's Chinese brand Liutech now sells in India and Southeast Asia.
- **3.5 How much time each kind of buyer leaves:**
  - contractors: none (the system has arrived);
  - factories: a few years;
  - robots: to about 2030;
  - plant engineers: longest.
  - Credit does not travel into these products, except as subscription for warehouse robots.
